# Sensitivity rules (genocide, mass atrocity and trauma topics)

## Before anything is drawn
- Open with a calm **content advisory** on dark paper (Khmer and English): what the film discusses, that it shows no graphic images, and a dedication to victims and survivors.
- The guides' first scenes carry the badge "FICTIONAL CHARACTERS · drawn from many survivors' stories", and the narrator says so.

## Pictures
- **No depiction of violence**: no blows, no bodies, no blood, no weapons aimed at people, no executions, no torture tools.
- **Absence stands for loss**: empty sandals on a road, broken spectacles on a book, banknotes in the wind, a chained pagoda gate, an empty barred room with one chair, a tin bowl of thin porridge, a candle.
- **Memorials only from outside.** Show the stupa, not the remains inside it. Exclude any photo with visible skulls or bones, even when the file says "memorial".
- **Victims' photographs** (for example S-21 prisoner portraits) need written permission from the holder; for S-21 that is the Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum via the Ministry of Culture and Fine Arts. If permission is granted: show them briefly, still and unaltered (no colour, crop, zoom or animation), inside a memorial frame. Without permission, use drawn empty frames and a candle. The film has to work without them.
- Do not use the museum's rules board or other objects whose text describes torture.
- Survivors' later-life photos (for example at a trial) are fine when openly licensed. Use `MF` (memorial frame), never the album style used for perpetrators.

## Words
- Narrate only ✓✓ facts. Give numbers that are estimates as ranges ("about 1.5 to 2 million") and add a method card.
- Name methods neutrally: "mapping mass graves", "population studies", "the tribunal".
- Use exact legal language: "found genocide was committed against… (trial level, not final)" versus "upheld on appeal".
- Keep contested events neutral (for example 7 January 1979): say who arrived and what ended, and leave "liberation" or "invasion" to the people speaking.
- The elder's memories are personal, quiet and non-graphic: separation, hunger, walking, a parent taken away "to study" who never came back, finding family again.
- Avoid myths and absolute rules (for example "everyone with glasses was killed"). Say "educated people were in great danger".

## Sound
- The hardest chapter (prisons, killing sites) is near-silent: no score, a single distant bell.
- No gunfire, explosion or scream SFX anywhere.
- The ending has gentle, hopeful tones as colour returns.

## After the render
- Recommend review by a native-speaking Khmer historian, and by a survivors' organisation (such as DC-Cam) if the film will be distributed publicly.
