# Democratic Kampuchea doc: fact-check, opening and chapters 1–5

Fact-checker: research agent (chapters 1–5). Compiled 2026-10-25. All sources accessed 2026-10-25.
Scope: the opening and chapters 1–5. Death toll, S-21, purges, the Vietnam war and the fall of DK belong to the ch.6–10 file.

**Key:** ✓✓ = two or more independent sources · ✓ (1) = one good source only · ⚖ DISPUTED = sources disagree (both versions given) · ✗ UNVERIFIED = do not use
**Page note:** "DCC p." numbers are the printed book page numbers (approximate; each PDF page holds two printed pages). "E313/E465 para" numbers are judgment paragraphs.
**Counting rule:** pages from the same publisher count as one source. DC-Cam's English book, Khmer book and slogan page count as one source (DC-Cam). The ECCC Trial Chamber judgments 002/01 and 002/02, their summaries and the ECCC timeline count as one source (ECCC). "X via Y" means I read Y, which cites X.

## DISPUTED POINTS (⚖)

1. **"Year Zero."**
   - (a) Popular and encyclopedia version: the KR "declared Year Zero" on 17 Apr 1975. This is Wikipedia "Year Zero (political notion)", which gives no scholarly support.
   - (b) Scholarly version: DK "never officially proclaimed a new chronology, nor did it ever introduce a new calendar". Its documents used ordinary dates. The phrase spread through Ponchaud's 1977 book title. Ers, citing Chon & Thet, *Behind the Killing Fields* (2010), says "Year Zero" was at most an informal KR expression for the emptying of the cities. Sources: ERS p. 8; PON title and ch. 12.
   - **Recommendation**: "what the French priest François Ponchaud called 'Year Zero'" or "a revolution later called 'Year Zero'". Never say "the regime declared Year Zero".
2. **Deaths during the April 1975 evacuation.**
   - Ieng Sary / Khieu Samphan: 2,000–3,000 (via E313 fn 1562; LOC calls this an understatement).
   - Kiernan: ~10,600 of 2 million (via E313 fn 1562).
   - Another estimate: 35,000 (via Short testimony, E313 fn 1562).
   - ECCC: evidence ranges from 2,000 to 20,000, and the court's finding is "at least several thousand" (E313 para 521).
   - **Use: "thousands died on the roads".**
3. **Phnom Penh's population before the evacuation.** 2–2.5 million (E313 para 520); 2.5 million (LOC 27.htm); "about two million" (DCC). Use "about two million or more".
4. **Phnom Penh's population during DK.**
   - DCC p. 17: "only about 40,000 inhabitants".
   - E313 para 518 and fn 1554: "up to 20,000"; one report gave ~20,000 in May 1976.
   - Ieng Sary's propaganda claims of 100,000 (1975) and 200,000 (1978) were rejected by the ECCC.
   - **Use "a few tens of thousands" or "a ghost city".**
5. **Rice ration figures.**
   - E465 paras 1008–1009: target ≈2 cans (~500 g) of rice/day; 13 thang ≈ 312 kg of **paddy**/year.
   - DCC p. 27: "312 kilograms of rice a year or 0.85 kg a day". This treats paddy as rice. Milled rice weighs much less than paddy (the exact milling ratio is not in my sources).
   - LOC 61.htm: target 570 g/day; actual 250–500 g depending on region.
   - **Don't give a single "promised" figure.** Say "the official ration was about two tins of rice a day, but most people got far less: often watery porridge".
6. **Zone code numbers.** North Zone: **303** (DCC p. 23) vs **304** (E313 para 215). Northeast: 108 (both), "later changed to 109" (E313 only). **Avoid the North Zone number on screen**, or say "North Zone".
7. **Constitution congress dates.** Draft presented 14 Dec 1975 (E313 para 233) vs approved 15–19 Dec 1975 (DCC p. 19). The 5 Jan 1976 promulgation is undisputed.
8. **Start of communal eating.** It varied by place: 1973–74 in some Southwest villages, 1975 or 1976 elsewhere, and general by 1977 (E465 fn 2883, 2890; DCC; LOC). Not a single date.
9. **Start of Trapeang Thma Dam construction.** "Between early 1976 and early 1977" (E465 para 1220). Witnesses also differ on completion (1977). Say "built in 1976–77".
10. **"Brother Number One" for Pol Pot.** Nuon Chea's "Brother No. 2" alias is confirmed by ECCC witnesses (E313 para 312), even though Nuon Chea denied using it. For Pol Pot, "Brother Number One" is widely used (Chandler's 1992 biography title; ERS). But I found no ECCC finding that it was an everyday DK-era name. Inside the party he was mostly "Brother" or by office (e.g. "870"; not verified). **Say "known as Brother Number One"** as a label, not as a quote of DK documents.
11. **Four-Year Plan years.** Correct: 1977–1980, drafted mid-1976 (DCC; E465, which cites the plan document's own title). ERS p. 8 wrongly says "1976–79", and the brief said "1976–80". Use **1977–1980**.

## UNVERIFIED: EXCLUDE (✗)

- "DK printed new banknotes but never issued them". Only coin and banknote dealers' catalogues say this.
- "The National Bank was blown up / dynamited". Only "destroyed" (Cambodian government press office) and "set on fire" (Ers citing Ponchaud generally) are sourced. The method is not verified, so say at most "the National Bank was destroyed".
- "Post and telephone services were abolished". No source found for public services. Use the sourced wording instead: "no public transport, no free movement".
- An exact clock time for the KR entry (witnesses vary from 6 to 9.30 a.m.). Say "morning".
- Sihanouk's second return date (31 Dec 1975).
- Any number of hospital patients who died.
- A pre-war average rice yield of "~1 t/ha", unless the other agent sources it.
- Any claim that the KR "killed everyone who wore glasses / spoke French" as a universal rule. Not in my sources. Use "educated people and former officials were in great danger" (DCC p. 26; E313 para 169).

## DO-NOT-SAY LIST

1. ❌ "The Khmer Rouge declared Year Zero / reset the calendar to zero." → ✅ "a revolution later called 'Year Zero'".
2. ❌ "Pol Pot was head of state." → ✅ Party Secretary and Prime Minister. Khieu Samphan was the (figurehead) head of state from April 1976; Sihanouk was head of state Sept 1975 – Apr 1976.
3. ❌ "King Sihanouk" for 1975–76 → ✅ "Prince Sihanouk, the head of state". The monarchy was formally replaced by the State Presidium in 1976.
4. ❌ "The Four-Year Plan of 1976–80" → ✅ "1977–1980" (written in 1976).
5. ❌ "Everyone in Phnom Penh cheered the Khmer Rouge." → ✅ Many cheered in relief, but many hid in fear. Some of the first "liberators" were the fake **Monatio** group of Hem Keth Dara, not the KR.
6. ❌ "Two million people were driven out in one day." → ✅ "within about a week".
7. ❌ "Angkar was Pol Pot" / "Angkar was a person." → ✅ "Angkar, 'the Organisation', was the secret party leadership".
8. ❌ "The Khmer Rouge" as the regime's own name. → ✅ The party was the Communist Party of Kampuchea (CPK). "Khmer Rouge" was Sihanouk's label from the 1960s. The CPK hid its existence until 27 Sept 1977.
9. ❌ "People were promised 0.85 kg / 1 kg of rice a day." → see Disputed 5.
10. ❌ "Tens of thousands died on the evacuation march" stated as fact. → ✅ "thousands".
11. ❌ "The US refused to rescue the Republic leaders." → ✅ The US offered evacuation; Sirik Matak and Long Boret chose to stay (DCC p. 13 quotes Sirik Matak's letter).
12. ❌ "The constitution guaranteed the CPK's leadership." → ✅ It did not mention the party at all.
13. ❌ "The 1976 election chose the government." → ✅ It was staged; the party had already chosen everyone.
14. ❌ Giving the North Zone a code number (see Disputed 6).
15. ❌ Calling the ECCC "the Khmer Rouge Tribunal judgment of 2014 convicted Pol Pot". → ✅ Pol Pot died in 1998 and was never tried. Case 002/01 convicted Nuon Chea and Khieu Samphan.
16. ❌ Saying all base people were safe or all regions were equally harsh. → ✅ Treatment varied by zone. For example, the Northwest was harsher than parts of the East early on (DCC p. 30).
17. ❌ Calling DK-era forced marriage "traditional arranged marriage". → ✅ The ECCC explicitly distinguished the two (E465-S para 40).
