## The death toll — what the numbers are and where they come from

Population in April 1975: **about 8 million** (✓✓ TK09, KIE03, HEU01).

| Estimate | Who / year | Method | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.3 million | PRK government committee, 1983 | Household survey + grave counts | Judged overstated by double counting (DC-MAP) |
| ~740,000 | Michael Vickery, 1984 | Population before/after minus normal deaths | Lowest scholarly figure (via DCC fn 1) |
| ~1 million | Early indirect studies, 1981–93 | Extrapolation from 1962 census | (via HEU01) |
| ~1.1–1.3 million remains in graves | DC-Cam / Craig Etcheson mapping, ~2000–2008 | GPS mapping of 19,733 pits; local estimates of bodies per pit (not exhumation counts) | Counts **mostly execution victims**, not deaths from hunger or disease. **This is where the "1.38 million" figure comes from**, but the exact "1,386,734" has no primary source we could find. |
| 1.67–1.87 million (21–24%) | Ben Kiernan, 2003 | Population before/after + community tolls | KIE03 |
| ~1.8–1.9 million | Marek Sliwinski, 1995 | Survey of ~1,300 households | via KIE03, HEU01 |
| 1.75–2.2 million, of which 0.8–1.3 million violent | Tabeau & Kheam, ECCC expert report, 2009 | Review of 12 estimates + censuses + 1993 voter list | TK09 (the ECCC later preferred not to rely on it) |
| 2.52 million (1.17–3.42) | Heuveline, 2001 | Cohort projection | **Covers the whole decade 1970–79**, not DK alone; his DK-only figure is about 2.2 million. **This is where "2.5 million" comes from.** |
| 1.2–2.8 million, median 1.9 million (21%) | Heuveline, 2015 | Stochastic reconstruction | HEU15 |
| **1.5–2 million "most probable"** | **ECCC Trial Chamber, 2018** | Review of all expert estimates | E465 para 297 |

**Recommendation:** Narrate *"Between about one and a half and two million people — roughly one in every five Cambodians — died under the regime: executed, or killed by hunger, overwork and disease."* Then one calm on-screen card explains the methods, mass-grave mapping (≈1.3 m remains), demographic studies (≈1.7–2.2 m) and the ECCC's view (1.5–2 m), so the film still shows where the 1.38 m and 2.5 m figures in your brief come from. Grave mapping counts only the dead found in graves, and the 2.5 m figure covers 1970–79, so neither works as the endpoint of the range.

## Disputed points (shown on screen as "Sources differ")

1. **Death toll** — see the table above. Range narrated as 1.5–2 million.
2. **"Year Zero"** — popular accounts say the regime "declared Year Zero"; scholarship (ERS; PON) shows DK never declared it or adopted a new calendar; the phrase spread through Ponchaud's 1977 book. → *"a revolution later called 'Year Zero'."*
3. **S-21 prisoner count** — ECCC minimum 12,273 (surviving lists) · DC-Cam ~14,000 · museum 15,000–20,000. → *"at least twelve thousand people, probably many more."*
4. **S-21 survivors** — 7 (media) · ~12 incl. 4 children (museum, DC-Cam 2007) · 202 incl. earlier "releases" (DC-Cam 2011; Duch said release lists were falsified). → *"only a handful survived."*
5. **Deaths on the evacuation** — 2,000–20,000 in ECCC evidence; Kiernan ~10,600; one estimate 35,000. → *"thousands."*
6. **Cham** — population before 1975 191,000 / 250,000 / 700,000; deaths 11,000 to 500,000; ECCC set no number. → no number narrated.
7. **Legal genocide findings** — Vietnamese: final. Cham: trial-level finding, not final (Nuon Chea died). → exact legal wording on screen.
8. **East Zone deaths 1978** — "as many as 100,000" (Dy) vs ~250,000 (Kiernan, via Wikipedia, book not opened). → *"tens of thousands."*
9. **Blue scarves** — worn by deportees (✓✓); Kiernan: a deliberate marker; Heder: not planned as one. → *"survivors remember them as the 'blue scarf people'."*
10. **The 1975 island clashes** — each government blamed the other; the ECCC did not decide. **1977 incursions** — reported by DC-Cam and LOC; Vietnam's larger claims not proven to the ECCC.
11. **Where the Front was founded (2 Dec 1978)** — Snuol, Kratie (DC-Cam; Cambodian government) vs Ho Chi Minh City preparatory meetings (ECCC). → date only, no place.
12. **Framing of 7 January 1979** — called "liberation" in Cambodian school texts (P2, P4, P5); described by others as a Vietnamese invasion that also ended the regime. → narrated neutrally: *"Vietnamese forces and Cambodian fighters of the Front reached Phnom Penh on 7 January 1979. The regime fell."* Survivors' own words (relief, return home) carry the feeling.
13. **Minor dates** — So Phim's death June (ECCC, S-21 notebooks) vs May 1978 (Dy); offensive 22 vs 25 Dec 1978; Choeung Ek 13 vs 15 km, stupa 1988 vs 1989; constitution congress 14 vs 15–19 Dec 1975. → the film uses the month or avoids the day.

## Approach for the film (for your approval)

- **Narration rule:** only ✓✓ facts are narrated; ⚖ points get a short "Sources differ" card; ✗ items stay out. Every narration line will carry its source code (as above) in `narration.py`.
- **Voices:** I recommend three voices: **Yeay Sophal** (an elderly woman, warm and slow) telling her memories in the first person, with only things the sources say happened to many people; **Malis** (a teenage girl) asking the questions; and a calm **adult narrator** for dates, numbers and the court findings, so no fictional character "testifies" to legal facts. A first-person survivor quote from DC-Cam or the ECCC record (e.g. Sim Soth on 17 April, or the "two or three days" testimony) can be read with credit, as a quotation, not as Sophal.
- **Both guides labelled fictional** on screen at their first appearance: "Yeay Sophal and Malis are fictional characters, drawn from many survivors' stories."
- **Victims' photographs:** S-21 prisoner portraits and Vann Nath's paintings are used **only if** the Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum grants written permission; shown still, briefly, unaltered, inside a memorial frame, never animated. Until then the film uses drawn empty frames and a candle, and works without them.
- **No graphic imagery** at all: empty shoes, abandoned books, broken spectacles, silent classrooms, a closed temple gate, a single candle; no weapons pointed at people, no bodies, no remains (Choeung Ek shown only as the stupa exterior at dawn).
- **Style and engine:** a new "memory drawing" engine (soft pencil and charcoal on aged paper; greyscale memory with smudges; warm colour for the present day; colour returning at the end), built from the charcoal/ink Khmer Republic engine so the timing, voice, subtitle and mixing pipeline stay proven.
- **Credits:** sources, scholarship, archive and licences only; **no "VOICES" credit**.
